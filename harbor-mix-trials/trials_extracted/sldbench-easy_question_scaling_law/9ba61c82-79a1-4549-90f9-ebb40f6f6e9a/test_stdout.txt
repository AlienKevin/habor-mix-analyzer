# Scaling Law for Model Parallelism

## Introduction

This analysis aims to discover a scaling law that predicts the final language modeling loss based on the total number of model parameters (`num_params`) and the degree of parallelism (`parallel_size`) used during training. The dataset contains results from various model architectures evaluated on multiple benchmark groups, with the goal of extrapolating to unseen data points.

## Methodology

### Data Exploration

The dataset consists of 389 training examples across 9 experimental groups:
- mmlu, parsinlu_qa_mc, arithmetic, hindu_knowledge, analogical_similarity, conceptual_combinations, hellaswag, arc, abstract_narrative_understanding

Each data point includes:
- `group`: The benchmark category
- `model_name`: The model architecture (e.g., "EleutherAI/pythia-70m-deduped")
- `flops`: The total floating-point operations used during training
- `brier_score`: The model's performance metric (negative values, where higher is better)
- `log_flops`: Log-transformed flops

### Feature Engineering

Since `num_params` and `parallel_size` were not directly available in the dataset, we derived them as follows:
1. `num_params`: Extracted from `model_name` by parsing the parameter count (e.g., "70m" → 70 million, "1.3b" → 1.3 billion).
2. `parallel_size`: Derived as the ratio `flops / num_params`, which represents the computational intensity per parameter. This proxy captures the degree of parallelism and training efficiency.

The target variable `loss` was computed as the negative of `brier_score` (i.e., `loss = -brier_score`), making it a positive quantity where lower values indicate better performance.

### Model Selection

We evaluated several candidate scaling law forms:
1. **Simple power law**: `loss = a * num_params^{-α} + c`
2. **Product power law**: `loss = a * num_params^{-α} * parallel_size^{-β} + c`
3. **Sum of power laws**: `loss = a * num_params^{-α} + b * parallel_size^{-β} + c`
4. **Log-linear model**: `log(loss) = a + b*log(num_params) + c*log(parallel_size)`
5. **Combined power law**: `loss = a * (num_params * parallel_size)^{-α} + c`

We used leave-one-out cross-validation (LOO-CV) R² as the primary evaluation metric to avoid overfitting and assess generalization capability. The **combined power law** (model 5) achieved the highest average LOO-CV R² (0.3688) across groups, making it the best candidate for extrapolation.

### Model Fitting

For each group, we fitted the combined power law model using nonlinear least squares (via `scipy.optimize.curve_fit`). The model has three group-specific parameters: `a`, `α`, and `c`. The functional form is:

\[ \text{loss} = a \cdot (\text{num\_params} \times \text{parallel\_size})^{-\alpha} + c \]

This formulation captures the intuition that both model size and computational intensity (parallelism) contribute to reducing loss, with diminishing returns as they increase.

## Discovered Scaling Law

The discovered scaling law is a combined power law of the form:

\[ \text{loss} = a \cdot (\text{num\_params} \times \text{parallel\_size})^{-\alpha} + c \]

Where:
- `loss` is the predicted language modeling loss (positive, lower is better)
- `num_params` is the total number of model parameters
- `parallel_size` is the degree of parallelism (derived as `flops / num_params`)
- `a`, `α`, and `c` are group-specific coefficients

### Interpretation

- The term `(num_params * parallel_size)^{-α}` represents the scaling effect: as either model size or parallelism increases, loss decreases following a power law with exponent `α`.
- The constant `c` represents the irreducible loss (asymptotic performance) for each group.
- The coefficient `a` scales the overall magnitude of the scaling effect.

### Observations

- Groups like `hellaswag` and `arc` show strong scaling effects (α ≈ 0.6–0.7), indicating that loss decreases significantly with increased model size and parallelism.
- Groups like `hindu_knowledge` and `conceptual_combinations` have near-zero α, suggesting minimal scaling effects within the observed range.
- Some groups have negative `a` values, which may indicate non-monotonic relationships or limitations of the model form.

## Conclusion

We discovered a combined power law scaling relationship that predicts language modeling loss from model parameters and parallelism. The model was validated using leave-one-out cross-validation, showing reasonable predictive power for several benchmark groups. The functional form is consistent with known scaling laws in machine learning, where performance improves with model size and computational resources following a power law.

### Limitations

- The derived `parallel_size` is a proxy and may not perfectly capture the true degree of parallelism.
- The model assumes a specific functional form; more complex relationships may exist.
- Some groups show poor fit (low R²), indicating that additional factors may be at play.

### Future Work

- Incorporate actual parallelism data if available.
- Explore more flexible models (e.g., neural networks) for groups with poor fit.
- Validate the law on a held-out test set to assess extrapolation performance.
