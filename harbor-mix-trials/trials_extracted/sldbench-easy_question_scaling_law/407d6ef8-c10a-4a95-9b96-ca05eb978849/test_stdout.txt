# Discovered Scaling Law

The dataset saved in `/app/data` contains the numeric inputs `flops` and `log_flops`, the grouping variable `group`, and the target `brier_score`. The fitted scale variable is

\[
x = \log_{10}(\mathrm{flops}).
\]

For every group I used the same quadratic law in log-compute:

\[
\widehat{\mathrm{brier\_score}}_g(x) = a_g + b_g x + c_g x^2.
\]

Because the stored score is the negative Brier score, physically plausible predictions should not be positive. The implementation clips the final prediction to `[-1, 0]`.

# Methodology

1. Loaded the dataset with `datasets.load_from_disk('/app/data')` and verified that `log_flops = log10(flops)`.
2. Treated `group` as selecting task-specific coefficients while keeping one shared functional form for all groups.
3. Compared simple candidate laws: group means, linear/quadratic/cubic polynomials in `log_flops`, and power-law variants in `flops`.
4. Selected the quadratic log-compute law because it gave a strong fit while remaining stable under held-out-row, held-out-model, and high-compute validation splits. Higher-degree curves slightly improved some random splits but extrapolated poorly; pure power laws were less stable for groups whose scores were nearly flat or noisy.
5. Refit the chosen law on all 389 visible rows. The resulting in-sample RMSE is about `0.0591` and MAE is about `0.0391` on `brier_score`.
