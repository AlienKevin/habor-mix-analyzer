# Scaling Law Analysis for Model Parallelism

## Mathematical Formula

The discovered scaling law predicts the Brier score (a language model evaluation metric based on proper scoring rules) as a quadratic function of the base-10 logarithm of FLOPs:

$$\text{brier\_score} = a_g + b_g \cdot \log_{10}(\text{flops}) + c_g \cdot (\log_{10}(\text{flops}))^2$$

where $a_g$, $b_g$, and $c_g$ are group-specific coefficients fitted from the training data.

This functional form generalizes the classic power-law scaling observed in neural language models. In standard scaling laws (Kaplan et al., Hoffmann et al.), the relationship between loss $L$ and compute $C$ follows $L(C) \propto C^{-\alpha}$, which manifests as a straight line in log-log space. The quadratic term $c_g \cdot (\log_{10}(\text{flops}))^2$ captures curvature arising from the approach to irreducible error -- as compute increases, the model's performance approaches a fundamental limit, causing the log-log relationship to bend. The quadratic polynomial is the simplest smooth extension that captures this saturation behavior.

### Physical Interpretation

In the context of parallel scaling, FLOPs serve as a proxy for total computational budget. The Brier score measures calibration quality (lower/more negative is better, with 0 being perfect). As FLOPs increase, the Brier score follows a curved trajectory in log space, reflecting the diminishing returns of additional compute -- a hallmark of scaling laws across machine learning.

### 2. Model Selection

I evaluated several candidate functional forms using least-squares fitting and leave-one-out cross-validation:

| Model Form | Parameters | Description |
|-----------|------------|-------------|
| Linear in log | 2 | $a + b \cdot \log_{10}(f)$ |
| Quadratic in log | 3 | $a + b \cdot \log_{10}(f) + c \cdot \log_{10}(f)^2$ |
| Inverse power law | 3 | $a + b \cdot f^{-c}$ (nonlinear) |
| Log + $1/\sqrt{f}$ | 3 | $a + b \cdot \log_{10}(f) + c / \sqrt{f}$ |
| Log + $1/\sqrt{f}$ + $1/f$ | 4 | Combined log and inverse terms |

**Selection criteria:**
- The functional form must be identical across all groups (problem constraint)
- Leave-one-out CV MSE indicates generalization quality
- Interpretability as a scaling law
- Model complexity (avoiding overfitting, especially for small groups)

The **quadratic in log** model was selected because:
1. It is the natural second-order expansion of any smooth scaling law in log space
2. It achieves competitive or best cross-validation performance on groups with clear signal (arc, hellaswag, arithmetic, parsinlu_qa_mc)
3. It correctly captures the curvature (saturation) that distinguishes real scaling laws from simple power-law fits
4. With only 3 parameters, it avoids overfitting the smaller groups

### 3. Coefficient Fitting

Coefficients were fitted independently per group using ordinary least squares regression. For a group $g$ with $n$ data points, we solve:

$$\min_{a,b,c} \sum_{i=1}^{n} \left( \text{brier}_i - (a + b \cdot x_i + c \cdot x_i^2) \right)^2$$

where $x_i = \log_{10}(\text{flops}_i)$. The optimization has a closed-form solution via the normal equations.
