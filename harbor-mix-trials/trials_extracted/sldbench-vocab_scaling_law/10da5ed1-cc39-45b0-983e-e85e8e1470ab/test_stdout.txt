# Discovered Scaling Law

## Mathematical Formula

Let
* `N` = `non_vocab_parameters` (number of non-embedding parameters)
* `V` = `vocab_size`
* `D` = `num_characters` (number of training characters)

The unigram-normalized loss is modelled by a Chinchilla-style additive
power-law in which the vocabulary size enters as a multiplicative
correction on each of the two reducible terms:

```
L(N, V, D) = E + A · V^{g1} · N^{-a} + B · V^{g2} · D^{-b}
```

This is a natural extension of the Hoffmann et al. (Chinchilla, 2022)
law `L = E + A/N^a + B/D^b`.  The vocabulary appears with two
separate exponents `g1, g2` because increasing `V`:

* slightly **lowers** the parameter-driven term (each parameter
  becomes more useful when the prediction problem is split into
  more output classes – `g1 < 0`), and
* slightly **raises** the data-driven term (each character carries
  more bits of information about which of the `V` tokens to emit
  – `g2 > 0`).

Because the loss is *unigram-normalized* (i.e. the unigram cross-
entropy has been subtracted) the irreducible term `E` is negative.

## Methodology

1. The dataset (1080 rows, single group `all_data`) was loaded with
   `datasets.load_from_disk` and converted to a `pandas.DataFrame`.
2. The structure of the design space was inspected: 9 vocab sizes,
   6 model sizes (non-vocab parameters), and ~20 different training
   data sizes per `(V, N)` cell.
3. Per-cell minimum losses showed that the asymptotic loss varies
   primarily with `N`, hinting at a Chinchilla-like form.
4. Several functional forms were fit by Nelder–Mead minimisation of
   the mean-squared error with many random restarts:
   * Plain Chinchilla `E + A/N^a + B/D^b`  →  MSE 0.0158, R² 0.975.
   * Vocab modulation on the `N`-term only        →  MSE 0.0107.
   * Vocab modulation on the `D`-term only        →  MSE 0.0089.
   * **Vocab modulation on both terms** (chosen)  →  MSE 0.0075,
     R² 0.988.
   * More flexible forms (extra additive `1/V^c`, or `E_0 + E_1·V^v`,
     etc.) gave no meaningful improvement (MSE essentially identical),
     so the simpler 7-parameter form was kept to avoid over-fitting
     during extrapolation.
5. The chosen form was re-fit with 300 random restarts to ensure a
   global optimum.

## Fitted Parameters

Only one experimental group is present in the data (`all_data`).
The fitted values are

| parameter | value          | meaning                          |
|-----------|----------------|----------------------------------|
| `E`       | `-6.20924`     | irreducible (unigram-normalised) loss |
| `A`       | `1.31297e+01`  | parameter-term amplitude         |
| `a`       | `0.08977`      | parameter-term exponent          |
| `B`       | `2.34411e+03`  | data-term amplitude              |
| `b`       | `0.38794`      | data-term exponent               |
| `g1`      | `-0.12470`     | vocab exponent on parameter term |
| `g2`      | ` 0.12553`     | vocab exponent on data term      |

Final training-set fit quality:
* MSE = 0.00752
* R²  = 0.9882

For any unknown group the law falls back to the parameters fitted on
`all_data`, but the *functional form* is the same for every group, as
required by the task.
